## Thesis

Adobe is a near-monopoly in professional creative software with 89% gross margins, a
30% ten-year median ROIC that has never dropped below 15%, and cash conversion of
1.5x net income. It was bought at 11.5x EV/EBIT and a 10.2% FCF yield on cost —
a multiple normally attached to a no-growth business — while owner earnings have
compounded at 18.6% a year for a decade.

The purchase is a bet that the market is pricing Adobe as if generative AI destroys
the creative-software franchise, and that it does not: that Adobe's distribution, file
formats, and enterprise contracts let it absorb AI as a feature rather than be
disintermediated by it. The valuation is the margin of safety — at a 10% FCF yield with
a shrinking share count, the investment works even at zero growth.

**What has to be true:** gross margin stays near 89%, ROIC stays above 20%, the share
count keeps falling, and revenue keeps growing at a high single digit or better. If AI
competition shows up, it will show up first in gross margin and net revenue retention,
not in the headline numbers.

## Quality tripwires

Entry values and the level at which each is considered breached. Two consecutive
quarters of any breach forces a written re-underwrite.

| Tripwire | At entry | Breach below/above | Screen threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| ROIC (TTM) | 55.3% | below 20% | — |
| ROIC minimum (10Y) | 14.8% | below 7% | ≥ 7% |
| ROIC years above 12% | 10 of 10 | below 7 | ≥ 7 |
| Gross margin (TTM) | 89.1% | **below 85%** | ≥ 35% |
| Gross margin 10Y std | 1.22pp | above 7pp | ≤ 7pp |
| Operating margin min (7Y) | 29.3% | below 20% | ≥ 5% |
| Cash conversion CFO/NI (TTM) | 1.50 | below 1.00 | ≥ 0.90 |
| Cash conversion median (10Y) | 1.50 | below 0.90 | ≥ 0.90 |
| Net debt / EBITDA | 0.11x | above 2.0x | ≤ 2.5x |
| Interest coverage | 34.8x | below 10x | ≥ 6x |
| Share count CAGR (5Y) | −3.52% | above 0% (dilution) | ≤ +1% |
| Owner earnings CAGR (10Y) | 18.6% | below 7% | ≥ 7% |
| Incremental ROIC (5Y) | 1.0 (capped) | below 0.12 | ≥ 0.12 |

The screen's own thresholds are far looser than the breach levels above — deliberately.
Adobe was bought for exceptional quality, not for barely clearing a bar. **Gross margin
is the tripwire that matters most**: it is where AI competition would show up first, and
85% is the level at which the pricing-power story is in question even though the screen
would still pass.

`iroic_5y = 1.0` is the documented cap for capital-light growth, not a measured 100% —
Adobe grew NOPAT while releasing capital. Read it as "comfortably above the bar", and
watch it fall off the cap rather than watch its level.

## Accounting-quality watch list

| Metric | At entry | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Accruals ratio | −0.124 | Healthy — cash flow well ahead of accounting earnings |
| SBC / FCF | 19.1% | High but normal for enterprise software; **~$2.03B/yr** |
| SBC / revenue | 8.05% | Watch for drift upward — it is a real cost paid in your ownership |
| Piotroski F-score | 6 / 9 | Middling; driven by the negative working capital and leverage sub-scores, not by profitability |
| Altman Z | 7.37 | No distress risk |
| Current ratio | 0.75 | Structurally below 1 — deferred revenue funds operations. Track the trend, not the level |
| Debt paydown years | 0.63 | Debt clearable in eight months of FCF |

**Buyback efficiency — the number to watch.** Adobe returned 11.5% of market cap
through net buybacks over the trailing year, but the share count fell from 433.0M
(2025-06-30) to 402.0M (2026-06-30), i.e. **−7.2%**. Efficiency = 7.2 / 11.5 = **63%**.
The missing ~37% is stock-comp issuance being mopped up. That is acceptable for a
software business but it must not deteriorate: if efficiency drops below ~50%, the
buyback has become a dilution treadmill and the headline shareholder yield is fiction.

Share count trend (`CommonStockSharesOutstanding`, quarter-end):
451.0M (2024-06) → 433.0M (2025-06) → 402.0M (2026-06) → 397.5M (latest).
Retirement is **accelerating**, which is the single most encouraging fact in the note.

## Manual inputs to collect each quarter

From the earnings release — the database cannot supply these, and for a subscription
business they lead the reported numbers.

- Remaining performance obligations (RPO) and deferred revenue, growth y/y
- Net revenue retention, and any change in disclosure of it
- Digital Media vs Digital Experience segment growth
- **AI product traction**: Firefly / AI-tier revenue disclosure, and whether AI features
  are priced as an upsell or bundled defensively (bundled = margin pressure coming)
- Guidance: raised / held / cut, and the stated reason
- Insider open-market purchases
- Competitive events: Canva, Figma, OpenAI/Google image and video tooling

## Sell triggers

Pre-committed. Position-specific overrides of the defaults in
[README.md](README.md#decision-rules).

**Sell on valuation** (thesis intact, price extreme)
- `fcf_yield_ev` falls below **4.5%** (EV/FCF above ~22x), or `ev_to_ebit` exceeds
  **25x**. From a 10.16% entry yield, that is roughly a doubling of the multiple.
- Trim, do not exit, if the compounding rate is still above 15% — a great business at a
  full price is a hold, not a sale.

**Sell on thesis** (price irrelevant)
- Gross margin below 85% for two consecutive quarters — the AI-disruption tell.
- Cash conversion (CFO/NI TTM) below 1.00 for two consecutive quarters.
- Share count rising year over year, or buyback efficiency below 50%.
- FCF per share below **$26.73** (the entry level) for four consecutive quarters.
- Net debt / EBITDA above 2.0x other than for a disclosed, single, value-creating
  acquisition.

**Add**
- `fcf_yield_ev` at or above **10.2%** with every tripwire intact.
